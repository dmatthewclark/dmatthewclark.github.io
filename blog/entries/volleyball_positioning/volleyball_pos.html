Volleyball Positions

Volleyball Rulebook

An essential resource for all volleyball players in Canada is the 2024-2025 Rulebook published by Volleyball Canada. This is the source for the following information. When paraphrased I will cite the exact rule number from the Rulebook.

Court Positions

Only six players can be in play on the court per team (Rule 7.3.1). Each player is assigned to a specific position on the court (Rule 7.4.1), which gets rotated clockwise after the team gains the right to serve (opponent gets a side-out; Rule 7.6.2). Before the service hit, the backrow players must be further back than the front-row player directly in front of them (Rule 7.4.2.1). Additionally, before the service hit the players must not cross their neightbour to the left or right (Rule 7.4.2.2).

Don't worry! You aren't locked to that location, you may move around to any position on the court after the service hit (Rule 7.4.4). However, a few restrictions apply to some positions. For instance, back-row players can not perform an attack hit in the front-zone if the ball is entirely above the top of the net when they make contact (Rule 13.2.3). This does not apply when they are ABOVE the front-zone. The players position is determined by their most recent contact with the floor (Rule 7.4.3), so just make sure you jump from the back-zone (Rule 13.2.2.1)! However, back-row players simply can not engage in blocking (Rule 14.6.2). Finally, it is only the back-right player that is allowed to serve (Rule 7.6.2, 12.2.2.2).

Player Positions

Setter

The decision-maker of the team. They determine the flow and positioning of attacks. Having a controlled and precise touch is essential.

Outside Hitter (Left-side Hitter)

Lead attacker. To be effective they should be able to jump high and adapt to various setting positions. They also tend to receive the opponents serve so they should be good at passing.

Opposite Hitter (Right-side Hitter)

Balance of attack and defence. These players have opportunities to attack but also need to be ready to defend against the opponents outside hitter.

Middle Blocker

First line of defence. They must be able to read the opponents setter and attacker to quickly get into the correct position. Quick reflexes are also required as they stand close to the setter who may setup a quick set.

Libero

The last line of defence. These players highly specialize in receiving an attack. The libero has specific rules they must follow, such as only being allowed to play in the back-row and being unable to serve. As a result, they typically swap with both middle blockers after they're finished in zone 1. This means the Libero only plays in zone 5 and 6.

Defensive Spetialist

Excell in quick reflexes and speed. They ensure the ball doesn't touch the ground.
